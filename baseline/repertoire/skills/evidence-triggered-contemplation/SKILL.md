# Evidence-triggered contemplation and retrospective revision

**Applicability:** Every new, substantive item of evidence, changed requirement, surprising observation, contradictory result, new domain fact, new constraint, or operational outcome. It can also be selected to challenge a previously stable understanding. No automatic code refactor is implied.

**Purpose:** Reassess what was previously believed, including what was *not* noticed, when new information arrives. New information changes the retrospective meaning of earlier assumptions, obligations and tests.

## Retrospective questioning (choose depth proportional to consequences)

- What is actually new? Which earlier observations, domain assumptions and inferences does it confirm, invalidate or qualify?
- Does it answer a known unknown, expose a previously unknown stakeholder or interface, or create new uncertainty?
- Is an obligation newly justified or now inapplicable? Has an earlier closed/verified obligation lost its supporting assumptions?
- Does the new knowledge alter the scope, oracle, or meaning of earlier tests? Should prior tests be rerun against **current** source?
- Would reverse reasoning, perturbation, try-to-break, changing representation, a controlled experiment, or *doing nothing* clarify the matter?
- Does a skill's applicability, evidence, version or status need revision? Would a new domain-specific skill be useful in future tasks?
- What can be recovered **within the authorized engineering window**? What remains open and why?

A simple datum that has no consequence may receive an explicit **NO_IMPACT** review with a short rationale. Meaningful contradictions require a distinguishing check or an honest unresolved/deferred status. Do not manufacture a change to reward contemplation.

## Evidence and temporal fidelity

Record source, checkpoint, artifact hash, current code SHA, affected prior assumptions/obligations/skills, new interpretation, executable checks, recovery attempt or reason recovery was inapplicable, and residual unknowns.

Distinguish historical proof about an old version from present verification. Prior checkpoint tests must be **rerun on the later implementation** as regression checks; their historical pass record is not transferable closure.

The review of new evidence must never use hidden benchmark evaluation results, private coordinator data, future task specifications, shared memory, or other arms' histories. Only authorized own-arm data may inform learning. A review mechanism cannot guarantee semantic completeness, only that the event was acknowledged.
