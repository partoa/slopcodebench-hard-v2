# Match reasoning to the actual need

Select a method from the unresolved question, the structure of the situation, and the evidence required. A keyword or familiar past solution is only a retrieval cue. It does not establish applicability or contract authority.

## Identify the gap

Distinguish these possibilities before prescribing an intervention:

- An expectation or affected consumer has not been identified.
- The expectation is known, but its authority or interpretation is unsettled.
- The situation is represented in a way that hides a relevant relationship.
- A known method was not retrieved or was applied outside its conditions.
- The necessary method is absent or inadequate.
- A valid decision is not realized through the operational system.
- The check or inference cannot distinguish fulfillment from a plausible failure.
- Attention or budget is allocated to a less consequential question.

These are hypotheses. Use a small distinguishing observation when several could explain the same outcome.

## Route by the question that needs answering

| Question | Candidate reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| What is owed, by whom and why? | Consumer outcomes, authority analysis, contract grounding |
| Which category owns this case? | Defining predicates, neighboring categories, overlap/gap and precedence analysis |
| What does this reference identify? | Structured reference semantics, context, composition and resolution |
| Are separately valid parts compatible? | Preconditions, relational constraints and dependency tracing |
| What changes or remains true through operations? | State transitions, invariants and data-flow reasoning |
| Does the promised behavior actually occur? | Operational path tracing and boundary-level verification |
| What depends on another actor or environment? | Control/observation separation and assumption-guarantee reasoning |
| What distinguishes competing explanations? | Counterexamples, controlled contrasts and causal diagnosis |
| Which response is proportionate? | Alternatives, tradeoffs, affected obligations and cost comparison |
| Why do available methods fail to fit? | Reframing, decomposition, reversal, analogy or method composition |

The companion skills in this package supply procedures for contract grounding, classification contracts, and structured references. Other methods may already be available in the task's authorized repertoire. Do not invent access to a tool or source simply because a method would benefit from it.

## Test applicability before applying

State the method's required inputs, assumptions, scope and expected product. Match those to observed task conditions. Look for a counterexample to the match, particularly a difference between this task and the earlier task that suggested the method. Prioritize by obligation significance, uncertainty, likely information gain and cost.

Use the smallest useful subset of methods. Combine them when different questions require different evidence; do not run a complete inventory by ritual. A provisional decision, targeted inspection or justified no-change outcome may be sufficient.

Check that the method yielded the promised product, not merely a document naming it. A taxonomy should distinguish cases; a reference should resolve; a verification criterion should detect plausible violations; a dependency trace should reach the actual outcome.

If the method is inadequate, revise the framing, adapt or compose methods, or investigate a new one within a stated budget. Preserve the observation and uncertainty. Record reusable learning with recognition cues, preconditions, exclusions and a transfer check. Do not turn a benchmark-specific expected answer into a universal rule or claim transfer from repeating the original example.
